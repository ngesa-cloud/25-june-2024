Nairobi, Kenya — a record of one day inside the wider Finance Bill 2024 protests

25.06.2024

The day tens of thousands of mostly young Kenyans marched on Parliament against the Finance Bill 2024 — and police opened fire. This page lays out what happened, hour by hour, and what is still disputed.

scroll
01 — Before the day

The Finance Bill 2024 was tabled in Parliament in May, proposing new taxes on bread, mobile money, motor vehicles and specialised hospitals, among other goods and services. Kenyans, organising largely online and led by a decentralised youth movement that came to be called Gen Z, began protesting the bill from the week it was published.

By 18 June, the government had dropped some of the most unpopular clauses, including the bread and cooking-oil levies. It wasn't enough. Protesters kept a running tally of "7 days of rage" and called for a total shutdown on 25 June, the day the bill was due for its third reading — the final vote before it would go to President William Ruto to sign.

the timeline

How the day unfolded

Compiled from contemporaneous reporting by Reuters, the BBC, Al Jazeera and CNN, and the Kenya National Commission on Human Rights' (KNCHR) 1 July statement. Times are approximate and reported inconsistently across sources — treat them as sequence, not a stopwatch.

Morning

Tear gas before the crowds arrive

Rights monitors later reported that police and plain-clothed officers in unmarked vehicles began firing tear gas toward assembly points before the bulk of protesters had gathered, setting a confrontational tone for the day ahead.

Morning deployment: Anti-riot officers deploying tear gas canisters through central Nairobi as volunteer medical workers navigate the perimeter. Photo: Capital News / CC.

Late morning

Crowds converge on the CBD

Marchers moved toward Nairobi's central business district from several directions, chanting against the bill and, increasingly, against the government itself. Parallel demonstrations were reported in Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret, Nakuru and other towns, and among Kenyans abroad in the US, UK and Germany.

Midday convergence: Thousands of unarmed demonstrators holding handwritten anti-tax placards and raising the national flag along central thoroughfares. Photo: Capital News / CC.

~1:00 pm

Parliament sealed off

As debate on the bill continued inside, police used barricades and tear gas to hold protesters back from Parliament's perimeter. For roughly three hours the crowd was held at bay while lawmakers moved toward a third-reading vote.

Perimeter defense: Armored water cannon vehicles discharging high-pressure chemical streams to suppress citizen banners outside Emperor Plaza. Photo: Capital News / CC.

~4:00 pm

The breach

Protesters broke through and entered the parliamentary grounds — the first time in the country's history that demonstrators had physically accessed Parliament. Parts of the complex were set alight. Police responded with live rounds. Multiple witnesses and journalists on the ground, including CNN's team, reported officers firing directly into the crowd outside the building.

The standoff: Unarmed demonstrators with hands raised facing an advancing phalanx of helmeted anti-riot police amidst blinding tear gas outside Parliament. Photo: Capital News / CC.

Afternoon into evening

Shootings outside Parliament

Several protesters were shot and killed on or near the parliamentary grounds. MPs were evacuated, reportedly through underground tunnels, as lawmakers passed the bill's third reading amid the chaos outside. A medical triage point set up nearby for the wounded was later reported to have been cleared with tear gas and rubber bullets.

The defiance: A demonstrator atop a street structure with arms raised in grief and defiance amidst heavy crimson tear gas and collapsed metal barriers. Photo: Capital News / CC.

Nationwide

Clashes beyond Nairobi

Protests and confrontations with police were also reported the same day in Nakuru, Uasin Gishu (Eldoret), Kajiado, Mombasa and Kisumu — counties that would later register among the highest death tolls in the KNCHR's cumulative count.

26 June

Ruto declines to sign the bill

The day after the storming, President Ruto announced he would not sign the Finance Bill 2024 into law, reversing course after weeks of insisting it was necessary. The bill was formally withdrawn shortly after.

01 • structural autonomy

Tribeless & Leaderless Architecture

By operating without a conventional steering committee, designated spokespersons, or ethnic patron chieftains, the youth rendered traditional state containment tactics useless. Intelligence agencies and political operatives found no central leadership to compromise, co-opt, or neutralize.

02 • nationwide footprint

Simultaneous Countywide Shutdowns

Protests were not confined to the capital. Millions marched simultaneously in Mombasa, Kisumu, Eldoret, Nakuru, Nyeri, Embu, Kisii, Kakamega, Kericho, and Garissa. The paralysis of commercial transport and administrative hubs overwhelmed regional security commands nationwide.

03 • open civic intelligence

Decentralized Digital Infrastructure

Using TikTok, X Spaces, and custom GPT bots, youths translated the 2024 Finance Bill into Swahili, Sheng, and vernacular dialects within 48 hours. Real-time geolocated dispatches tracked tear gas salvos, unmarked abduction vehicles, and water cannon deployments to protect peaceful marchers.

04 • direct mutual aid

M-Pesa Citizen Crowdfunding

Rejecting corporate and political donations, citizens pooled micro-donations directly via M-Pesa. Millions of shillings were disbursed in real time to post bail for thousands detained in police cells, purchase emergency medical supplies, and fund trauma surgeries for live-fire casualties.

05 • frontline medical corps

Volunteer Cathedral & Street Triage

Doctors, nurses, and medical students formed volunteer medical corps under fire, converting Holy Family Basilica, Jamia Mosque, and All Saints Cathedral into rapid-response field hospitals to treat tear gas asphyxiation, blunt blast trauma, and gunshot wounds.

06 • civic discipline & sanitation

Street Cleanliness & Constitutional Order

Demonstrators carried trash bags throughout marches, sweeping broken glass and clearing debris behind protest columns to demonstrate that the movement was rooted in civic accountability, constitutional rights, and patriotic defense, rather than wanton destruction.

International Record: "Protests Erupt Across Major Towns in Kenya"

"Thousands of Kenyans, predominantly young men and women, flooded the streets of Nairobi and other cities once again... chanting 'Stop killing us' and 'End police impunity'... Roads leading into the Central Business District were barricaded by dawn. Razor wire and heavily armed officers sealed off all approaches to State House and Parliament, signaling a government on high alert."

International coverage highlighted how simultaneous countrywide mobilizations stretched state policing resources to breaking point. Security forces barricaded city centres at dawn and cordoned government installations behind military-grade razor wire, demonstrating the unprecedented leverage exercised by unarmed civilian youth against entrenched executive authority.

the toll

Nobody agrees on a number

Death tolls for 25 June specifically — as opposed to the full 18 June–August protest period — vary by source, timing and methodology. The figures below are what each body reported closest to the event; later cumulative reports pushed totals for the wider protest movement well past 60.

19
Deaths in Nairobi, 25 June
Police / early wire reports
39
Deaths, protests to date — 1 July
KNCHR statement
61
Deaths across all 2024 protests
Amnesty Kenya & rights coalition, Sept. 2024

The KNCHR's 1 July figure also recorded at least 361 injuries and 39 arrests connected to the protests, and separately noted six protesters shot dead specifically outside Parliament on 25 June. By September, a coalition including Amnesty International, Article 19, the Defenders Coalition, IMLU, the Law Society of Kenya and the Kenya Medical Association had documented 61 deaths and 72 reported abductions or disappearances tied to the year's protests. Figures were revised again around the first anniversary in June 2025, with Amnesty Kenya citing at least 60 deaths and 20 people still missing.

what came after

Aftermath

  • 26 Jun 2024

    President Ruto declines to sign the Finance Bill; it is withdrawn days later.

  • Jul–Sep 2024

    Rights organisations begin documenting abductions of activists and protest organisers, alongside the death toll from June and July demonstrations.

  • 25 Jun 2025

    On the first anniversary, thousands return to the streets. Parliament and State House are ringfenced with razor wire; police fire tear gas in Nairobi. Marches in Mombasa and Nakuru remain peaceful. Families and opposition leaders lay wreaths outside Parliament.

  • Ongoing

    As of the 2025 anniversary, Amnesty Kenya and families of the dead say accountability for the shootings — and for dozens of reported enforced disappearances — remains largely absent. No senior officer is known to have been convicted over the 25 June shootings.
